Mathematical Intuition

Why Strictly Concave Functions Drive Purer Splits

Any valid binary split partitions a parent node with probability $p$ into left and right children $(p_L, p_R)$ with weights $w_L + w_R = 1$ such that the expectation is conserved:

$p = w_L p_L + w_R p_R$  ⟹  $\Delta I = I(p) - \big[w_L I(p_L) + w_R I(p_R)\big]$

Geometrically, $w_L I(p_L) + w_R I(p_R)$ is the height of the straight secant chord connecting $(p_L, I(p_L))$ and $(p_R, I(p_R))$ evaluated at $p$. By Jensen's Inequality:

Shannon Entropy
$|H''(p)| = \frac{1}{p(1-p)\ln 2}$

Curvature explodes to $\infty$ near $p \to 0, 1$. Aggressively rewards isolating small pure buckets.

Gini Impurity
$|G''(p)| = 4$ (Constant)

First-order Taylor approximation of Entropy ($\ln p \approx p-1$). Uniformly concave parabola.

Misclass Error
$|E''(p)| = 0$ ($p \neq 0.5$)

Piecewise linear. Zero gain unless children cross the $p=0.5$ majority boundary!

01

Why ID3 / C4.5 Entropy Minimization = Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

Suppose a leaf node $m$ contains $N_m$ training points with class counts $N_{m,c}$. If we model the leaf's labels with a Categorical distribution $\theta_m = (\theta_{m,1}, \dots, \theta_{m,C})$, the log-likelihood of the data in leaf $m$ evaluated at the MLE $\hat{\theta}_{m,c} = \frac{N_{m,c}}{N_m}$ is:

$\ell(\hat{\theta}_m) = \sum_{c=1}^{C} N_{m,c} \log_2 \hat{\theta}_{m,c} = - N_m \sum_{c=1}^{C} \hat{\theta}_{m,c} \log_2 \hat{\theta}_{m,c} = - N_m \, H(\hat{\theta}_m)$

Therefore, choosing a split that minimizes the weighted child entropy $\sum_{m} \frac{N_m}{N} H(\hat{\theta}_m)$ is mathematically identical to greedy minimization of the Negative Log-Likelihood (Cross-Entropy Loss) of a piecewise-constant probabilistic classifier!

02

Information Gain as Expected KL-Divergence (Posterior vs. Prior)

Before splitting, our prior belief over class $Y$ in the parent node is $P(Y)$. After observing feature split $X=x$, our posterior belief in child $x$ is $P(Y \mid X=x)$. Information Gain is the Mutual Information $I(Y; X)$, which equals the expected Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence from the prior to the posterior:

$\text{IG}(Y, X) = H(Y) - H(Y \mid X) = \mathbb{E}_{X}\Big[ D_{\text{KL}}\big( P(Y \mid X) \;\parallel\; P(Y) \big) \Big]$

Intuition: A split has high Information Gain if and only if knowing which branch $X$ a sample falls into causes a large information-theoretic shift ($D_{\text{KL}}$) from the parent distribution $P(Y)$ to the child distribution $P(Y \mid X)$.

03

Probabilistic Meaning of CART's Gini Impurity: Stochastic Labeling Error

Instead of deterministic majority voting ($\arg\max_c p_c$, which gives Misclassification Error $1 - \max_c p_c$), imagine a stochastic classifier that draws a random label $Y_{\text{pred}} \sim \text{Categorical}(p_1, \dots, p_C)$ according to the leaf's empirical probabilities. What is the probability that a randomly arrived test point $Y_{\text{true}} \sim \text{Categorical}(p_1, \dots, p_C)$ is misclassified?

$P(Y_{\text{pred}} \neq Y_{\text{true}}) = \sum_{c=1}^{C} p_c (1 - p_c) = 1 - \sum_{c=1}^{C} p_c^2 = \text{Gini}(p)$

Equivalently, if we encode class $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ and fit a regression tree minimizing Mean Squared Error (Brier Score) $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i (y_i - \hat{p})^2$, the variance of a Bernoulli leaf is $\hat{p}(1-\hat{p}) = \frac{1}{2}\text{Gini}(\hat{p})$. Thus, CART Gini splitting is identical to variance reduction on one-hot encoded targets!
